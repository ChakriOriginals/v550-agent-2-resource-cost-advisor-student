# Scenario 1 Background

## After the Merger One Building Nine Desks

Everything after the real March 2022 merger announcement is fictional course material. Do not research or import facts about the real organizations.

Meridian Arts Collective combined New Play Development, Mainstage, and Learning and Media. The organization has 11 full-time staff, about 30 contract artists per season, three legacy artistic leads, and Executive Director Dana Okoye. Marcus Feld, Director of Operations, leads the Facilities Plan with Priya Raghavan and Tomas Beltrán.

The merger plan was a $90,000, 14-month, 214-page consultant plan. Its facilities section was not implemented. A 2019 space-utilization study contains floor plans, room dimensions, and one year of booking data. A website consolidation finished four months late, and a donor-database merge was abandoned without a written record.

The project team is 0.75 FTE combined: Marcus is 0.4 FTE while managing front of house, rentals, and the maintenance contract; Priya is 0.2 FTE while teaching two classes; and Tomas is 0.15 FTE while running the season. Tomas has worked in the Waldron for 11 years. Ruth Adeyemi chairs the Board Facilities Committee, negotiated the original lease, attends few meetings, and answers email within a day. Gwen Tsai is the City Arts Commission liaison and determines how strictly community-access documentation is audited.

The team plans allocation of the approximately 38,000-square-foot John Waldron Arts Center: Ruth Auditorium, Firebay black box, two galleries, Studios A through F, scene shop, and a nine-desk admin suite. The plan must account for the City lease's 900 annual hours of below-market community access, the dance school's fixed Studios C and D agreement through August 2028, the gallery calendar contracted 14 months out, fire code, and ADA access.

Ruth Auditorium has 232 fixed seats and a full fly system. Firebay is an 87-seat flexible black box. The Main Gallery is 2,100 square feet, the Corridor Gallery is 900 square feet, the six studios are 400–800 square feet each, and the shared basement scene shop is 1,800 square feet. The building has one loading dock and one freight elevator.

New Play Development wants Firebay year-round for workshops. Mainstage wants Ruth plus Firebay rehearsal access during tech weeks. Learning and Media wants four studios on a fixed weekly schedule and is the only program whose enrollment grew. Six community groups rent studios monthly without written agreements. The admin suite has nine desks for 11 full-time employees. Meridian currently documents 515 of the required 900 annual community-access hours.

The $35,000 cash budget covers planning costs, not staff labor. The board votes May 14, 2027, and the season is announced June 1. The project manager controls the meeting schedule, consultation, recommendation format, and cash budget, but not the board's allocation decision, legacy-lead participation, the season calendar, or lease terms.

Nobody has been assigned responsibility for fallout when a legacy lead is unhappy or for speaking to the City, and Dana has said she will not referee between the legacy leads.

Out of scope: renovation, construction, capital campaign, or equipment purchases; staff restructuring, layoffs, or hiring; ticketing-system merger; lease renegotiation; and choosing the 2027–28 season shows.
